You'll teach a computer to recognise things through your camera, in your browser. No code, no sign-up, no expensive laptop.

Watch it once (90 seconds)

Your 10 minutes

  1. Open Teachable Machine in a new tab It's a free Google tool. Choose Image Project, then Standard image model.

    Open Teachable Machine

  2. Make 2 classes Rename "Class 1" and "Class 2" to two things you can show the camera. Some ideas:
    • Naira note / Not money
    • Ripe plantain / Unripe plantain
    • Thumbs up / Thumbs down
    • Pen / Phone
  3. Give it about 30 photos of each Click Webcam and hold the button to snap photos. Move the object around: closer, further, different angles.
    On a phone, or no webcam? Click Upload instead and pick photos from your gallery.
  4. Click Train model Keep the tab open while it trains. It takes under a minute.
  5. Test it live Show it each object in the Preview panel and watch the bars move. Then try to fool it: show it something new.
  6. Come back here Read what just happened, then show the world what you built.

What just happened

You gave it examples. You didn't write any rules like "plantain is yellow". You showed it photos with the right answer attached. That's the core of machine learning: a computer learns from examples, not instructions.

It found patterns. During training, the model looked for what makes your class 1 photos different from your class 2 photos: colours, shapes, edges. It started from a model already trained on millions of photos, which is why 30 of yours were enough.

It made predictions, and sometimes got them wrong. When you showed it something new, it guessed which class looked most similar. It can be fooled by a messy background, poor light, or anything it never saw in training. Real AI fails the same way, which is why the data you train it on matters so much.
